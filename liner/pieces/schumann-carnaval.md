# Overview

A masked ball in twenty-one short pieces. Commedia dell'arte figures (Pierrot, Harlequin, Pantalone and Columbine) dance beside Schumann's friends: the young pianist Clara Wieck as *Chiarina*, his fiancée of the time, Ernestine von Fricken, as *Estrella*, and portraits of [[chopin|Chopin]] and Paganini. [[schumann|Schumann]] himself comes twice, as the dreamy *Eusebius* and the fiery *Florestan*, the two characters he signed his music criticism with.

The four notes of the subtitle are the letters A, S, C, H, which in German note names spell A, E flat, C, B, or A flat, C, B. Asch was Ernestine's home town in Bohemia; the same letters are in *Fasching*, German for carnival, and in SCHumann. Between two of the pieces Schumann printed the three spellings as *Sphinxes*, riddles in long notes that pianists usually do not play.

Schumann wrote it in 1834–35, using the opening of an unfinished set of variations on a Schubert waltz for the *Préambule*, and published it in 1837 with a dedication to the violinist Karol Lipiński. It ends with the *March of the League of David against the Philistines*, his imaginary league of artists against the narrow-minded, in triple time.

This file is a performance from the 2004 International Piano-e-Competition, recorded on a Yamaha Disklavier (MAESTRO dataset; the pianist is not named). At 28 minutes it is the longest of the four MAESTRO performances, and it has about 3,500 more notes than the others, most likely because it takes more of the written repeats.

source: Robert Schumann, *Carnaval*, Op. 9, first edition (Breitkopf & Härtel, 1837); John Daverio and Eric Sams, "Schumann, Robert," *Grove Music Online*; MAESTRO v3.0.0 (Google Magenta)

# Sections

## @0:00 1. Préambule
A grand opening in A flat, then faster and faster.

## @2:13 2. Pierrot
The sad clown, soft and halting, with sudden loud notes.

## @3:57 3. Arlequin
Harlequin leaps across the keyboard.

## @5:01 4–10. From Valse noble to the Lettres dansantes
Valse noble, Eusebius, Florestan, Coquette, Réplique, Papillons and the dancing letters A.S.C.H.–S.C.H.A.

## @12:54 11. Chiarina
Clara Wieck, passionate, in C minor.

## @14:13 12. Chopin
A nocturne in Chopin's style, marked Agitato.

## @15:33 13. Estrella
Ernestine von Fricken, in F minor.

## @15:55 14. Reconnaissance
Recognition: a melody over fast repeated notes.

## @17:42 15. Pantalon et Colombine
The old man and Columbine, a quick comic scene.

## @18:36 16. Valse allemande, with Paganini
A German waltz, interrupted by a fiendish portrait of the violinist Paganini.

## @20:10 The waltz again
The Valse allemande returns after Paganini.

## @21:06 17. Aveu
A confession.

## @22:08 18. Promenade
A stroll arm in arm, in D flat.

## @24:31 19–20. Pause, then the March of the League of David
The opening's rush returns and leads straight into the final march.

## @2:13 · listen
Almost every piece is built on four notes, from the letters A, S, C, H.

In German note names S is E flat (Es) and H is B, so the letters spell A, E flat, C, B, or with As, A flat, C, B.

screen: Four notes | The letters A, S, C, H, read as notes.

## @18:36 · trivia
In the middle of a gentle German waltz, the violinist Paganini bursts in, fast and wild.

## @24:31 · analysis
The League of David's "march" is in triple time, not the two or four of a march.

Schumann mocks the Philistines with the old *Grandfather's Dance*, which he had used before in *Papillons*.

screen: Against the Philistines | A march in three: the artists rout the narrow-minded.

source: Daverio and Sams, *Grove Music Online*
