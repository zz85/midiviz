# Overview

The twelfth guest at [[schumann|Schumann]]'s masked ball in [[carnaval|*Carnaval*]] is a real person: [[chopin|Frédéric Chopin]]. Schumann writes him a portrait in his own style, a singing melody high in the right hand over wide left-hand arpeggios held in the pedal, like one of Chopin's nocturnes. It is marked *Agitato*, restless, and lasts little more than a minute.

Schumann had admired Chopin since 1831, when his first published article greeted Chopin's Variations, Op. 2, with "Hats off, gentlemen, a genius!" The two composers were born the same year.

## @0:08 · history
In 1831 Schumann's first published article hailed Chopin's Variations, Op. 2.

Its most quoted line is Eusebius's: "Hats off, gentlemen, a genius!" Schumann wrote his reviews in the voices of two imagined characters, Florestan and Eusebius.
