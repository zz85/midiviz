# Overview

A 27-bar piece written to test sight-reading at the Paris Conservatoire. That practical origin helps explain its concentrated challenges: exposed melody, changing textures, wide arpeggios, inner voices, octave writing and flexible tempo, all inside roughly a minute and a half.

[[ravel|Ravel]] begins with a single unaccompanied line. The left hand enters only in bar 2; later the melody expands into octaves, then returns high above a quieter close.

source: Ravel, *Prélude*, Durand first edition (1913); Bibliothèque nationale de France work authority; Philharmonie de Paris programme archive

# Sections

## m1 Melody alone
The right hand enters after a rest, without accompaniment.

## m2 Harmony arrives
The left hand quietly places space beneath the line.

## m10 Melody in octaves
The opening contour returns enlarged across two registers.

## m16 Chordal answer
Rolled and closely voiced chords interrupt the octave texture.

## m20 Expansion
The harmony and register broaden toward the piece's fullest sonority.

## m23 Final slowing
The music descends through a second *ralenti* toward the last high phrase.

## m27 Très lent
Two fermatas suspend the ending.

# Moments

## m1-m5 · listen
The opening melody is alone for almost a bar. Its first contour will return near the end.

screen: One line alone | The accompaniment waits until the second bar.

## m10-m15 · analysis
The melody returns in octaves, turning an intimate line into the piece's broad central span.

## m16-m19 · listen
Rolled chords answer the melody. Their colour matters more than their volume.

## m23-m27 · technique
The final bars ask for two degrees of slowing: *ralenti*, then *très lent*.

screen: The last five bars | Ravel slows twice before the final fermata.
