# Overview

A 32-bar sarabande whose quiet bass supports one of Bach's most ornamented melodies. The thirty Goldberg Variations do not principally vary that melody: they preserve this Aria's bass and harmonic plan, then build new dances, canons and virtuoso pieces over it.

The Aria divides into two equal halves, each repeated here. The first travels from G major to D major; the second ranges more widely before finding its way home. Nearly every bar carries an ornament, but the slow triple-time dance remains audible underneath: weight on the second beat, a measured step in the bass, and a cadence every few bars.

Bach published it in 1741 at the beginning of the fourth volume of his *Clavier-Übung*. He asks for a two-manual harpsichord in the collection's title, though the Aria itself needs only one. This MIDI comes from a Mutopia LilyPond edition, with written ornaments realized and rule-based dynamics added; it is a score interpretation, not a captured performance.

source: Bach-Archiv Leipzig, “Introduction” to the Goldberg Variations; Bach, first edition (Nuremberg, 1741); Mutopia Project, “Goldberg Variations — Aria”

## @0:06 · analysis
The graceful melody is written in the style of a sarabande, a slow dance in triple time.

A sarabande often gives expressive weight to beat two. Bach lets ornaments delay and decorate the upper voice while the bass keeps the dance poised.

source: Bach-Archiv Leipzig, “Introduction”
