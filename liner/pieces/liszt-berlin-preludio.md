# Overview

Not a one-minute prelude but an eight-second [[album-leaf|album leaf]] of only two bars. Seven rolled [[diminished-seventh|diminished-seventh chords]] rise in the right hand and fall in the left, marked *precipitato*; the second bar cuts them off with a compact C-major cadence.

The surviving leaf is signed and dated Berlin, 3 March 1842. The title does not describe a Berlin scene: it identifies the place where Liszt wrote the souvenir.

source: manuscript and Aidan Modica's CC0 edition from the manuscript, IMSLP #710207

# Sections

## m1 Seven rolled chords
The hands move in contrary directions through a chain of symmetrical chords.

## m2 C-major cadence
The rush stops: two clipped chords and one final C-major chord.

# Moments

## m1 · analysis
Each chord divides the octave into minor thirds while the hands move apart.

## m2 · listen
After seven unstable sonorities, a very ordinary C-major cadence sounds unexpectedly decisive.
