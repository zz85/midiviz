# Overview

Two bars of softly turning triplets establish the atmosphere before the melody enters. Those triplets persist through most of the piece, supporting a line that grows from a single voice into octaves and then dissolves into rapid descending figures.

[[lyadov|Lyadov]] was drawn to concentrated forms, but this prelude is the longest work in the season: just over two minutes at its measured *Moderato*. Its scale comes from repetition, register and carefully placed swells rather than a new idea in every phrase.

source: Lyadov, Three Pieces Op. 11; *Complete Piano Works*, vol. 1, eds. Igumnov, Lamm and Oborin (Muzgiz, 1947), pp. 132–134

# Sections

## m1 Triplets alone
The left hand turns quietly for two bars before the melody appears.

## m3 First melody
An accented, broadly slurred line enters over the continuous motion.

## m19 Octave expansion
The melody widens into octaves and climbs through a longer crescendo.

## m31 Falling sequence
Triplets spill down through several registers.

## m41 Opening returns
After a ritardando, the first melody and tempo return.

## m57 Second expansion
The octave idea comes back higher and more strongly.

## m69 Dissolving coda
Another falling sequence empties the upper register.

## m75 Final sweep
A single arpeggio rises across the keyboard before the last B-minor chord.

# Moments

## m1-m3 · listen
The accompaniment begins alone. The melody's delayed entrance makes its first accent feel inevitable.

## m19-m30 · analysis
The original line expands into octaves while the same triplet current continues underneath.

## m31-m40 · listen
The melody breaks into descending triplet chains, then slows toward silence.

## m40-m41 · technique
The first *ritardando* must release cleanly into *a tempo*: the opening pulse returns at once.

screen: Ritardando, then return | The first tempo comes back with the first melody.

## m57-m68 · listen
The octave expansion returns in a higher register and reaches the strongest dynamic of the prelude.

## m75-m78 · technique
The closing arpeggio passes through both staves and nearly the whole keyboard before the final chord.
