---
title: Diminished seventh chord
kind: term
---

Four notes, each a minor third above the last, such as C♯–E–G–B♭. Because the chord divides the octave into four equal parts, it sounds the same in every inversion, and there are only three different ones.

It is unstable and can resolve to several keys. Composers use it to pull toward a new key or to make the strongest tension in a passage. Bach's Prelude in C uses one in bars 12, 14, 22, 23 and 28; the coda of the Moonlight Sonata's finale strikes two in a row as its climax.
