# One-Minute Piano Discoveries

A working list for YouTube Shorts made with `032_pianist.html`: complete piano miniatures around one minute, biased toward music pianists may recognize but general audiences do not hear constantly.

This is a living production document. Durations are those of the current MIDI when one exists, otherwise a normal-performance estimate. A public-domain composition does **not** make somebody else's MIDI performance public domain; every imported or generated file needs its own source and licence.

## Selection rules

Prefer pieces that:

- last about 45–90 seconds as a complete musical thought;
- have a clear idea in the first three seconds;
- create legible hand motion and at least one visual change for the portrait director;
- are not the composer's standard general-audience hit;
- have a public-domain score and a MIDI we can legally synchronize with video;
- end on a real cadence rather than an arbitrary 60-second cut.

Avoid making every title a claim that a work is “unknown” or a “hidden masterpiece.” The discovery should be concrete: an unusual meter, a one-page manuscript, a dialogue between the hands, or a miniature form.

### Why this slate may fit better

- It sounds like one curated recital rather than eight demonstrations of speed or technique.
- Every piece can support a complete Short with room to breathe.
- The visual variety comes from light, posture, touch and harmony—not increasingly elaborate costumes or effects.
- Grieg, Ravel, Scriabin and Schumann provide searchable composer names; Beach, Chaminade and Lyadov give the channel a genuine discovery identity.
- The tradeoff is less instant spectacle. Open each Short on a beautiful hand or face composition and let one precise listening cue provide the hook.

## Season visual and copy board

All final exports: YouTube Short 9:16, 1080×1920, 60 fps. Preview first at 540×960. Use a complete piece unless the table explicitly says otherwise.

### 1. Grieg — Elves' Dance

- **Visual:** `braid-halter`, champagne satin, cel look, pale-gold light, spotlight dust, no depth of field. Single frame; favor hands, keyboard-up, player view and low right.
- **Listening cue:** “Clipped chords answer scurrying staccato.”
- **Title:** **Grieg's Elves' Dance in One Minute | Op. 12 No. 4**
- **Description:** Edvard Grieg's *Elves' Dance*, Op. 12 No. 4, answers clipped chords with quick staccato figures. Champagne satin, cel outlines and dust in the spotlight give the performance a pale-gold nocturnal setting. #Shorts #piano

### 2. Schumann — An Important Event

- **Visual:** `ponytail-qipao`, red and gold, stylized look, ceremonial light and moderate vignette. Stacked 60/40: frontal pianist above, hands and hammers below.
- **Listening cue:** “A grand dotted march, compressed into a miniature.”
- **Title:** **A Tiny March with Very Big News | Schumann**
- **Description:** Robert Schumann's *An Important Event*, Op. 15 No. 6, compresses a ceremonial dotted march into less than a minute. Red-and-gold staging places the pianist above the hands and hammers below. #Shorts #piano

### 3. Scriabin — Prelude Op. 11 No. 14

- **Visual:** `long-gown`, red velvet, realistic look, dark crimson light, grain and strong vignette. Fast single-frame director; hands, pedals, low right and one face shot.
- **Listening cue:** “Fifteen eighth notes drive every restless bar.”
- **Title:** **What Does 15/8 Feel Like? | Scriabin Prelude**
- **Description:** Alexander Scriabin's Prelude in E-flat minor, Op. 11 No. 14, surges through a Presto 15/8 meter. Red velvet, low angles and restrained film grain turn its irregular pulse into a compact stage storm. #Shorts #piano

### 4. Tchaikovsky — The New Doll

- **Visual:** `shift-long`, blush pink, stylized look, warm ivory light and soft bloom. Stacked 60/40: pianist above and first-person lesson view below.
- **Listening cue:** “Each phrase climbs, then starts higher.”
- **Title:** **The New Doll Comes to Life | Tchaikovsky Op. 39 No. 9**
- **Description:** Tchaikovsky's *The New Doll*, Op. 39 No. 9, unfolds through bright, buoyant B-flat-major phrases. Blush fabric and a first-person lesson view present the keyboard as a softly lit toy theatre. #Shorts #piano

### 5. Alkan — Le Staccatissimo

- **Visual:** `bob-bodycon`, gold sequins, sharp cel look, cool white and brass light. Stacked 40/60 with a top-down lesson view; keep the keyboard sharp.
- **Listening cue:** “Repeated notes above, racing scales below.”
- **Title:** **One Minute of Alkan | Le Staccatissimo**
- **Description:** Alkan's *Le Staccatissimo*, Op. 63 No. 2, places repeated right-hand notes above rapid left-hand scales. Gold sequins and a top-down lesson view expose the two contrasting kinds of motion. #Shorts #piano

### 6. Bach — Prelude in F major

- **Visual:** `braid-qipao`, ivory and navy, realistic look and clean neutral light. Stacked 50/50 with hands below; include hammers and keyboard-up.
- **Listening cue:** “Arpeggios and trills pass between the hands.”
- **Title:** **Bach's Other F-Major Prelude | BWV 856**
- **Description:** Bach's Prelude in F major, BWV 856, passes flowing arpeggios and long trills between the hands. Clean ivory-and-navy staging keeps the keyboard and its interlocking movement at the center. #Shorts #piano

### 7. Liszt — Berlin Preludio

- **Visual:** `bob-cocktail`, black satin, warm sepia, subtle grain. One fixed manuscript-like composition rather than a normal director sequence.
- **Listening cue:** “Seven rolled chords rush into a C-major cadence.”
- **Title:** **Liszt Fit This Prelude into Two Bars | S. 164g**
- **Description:** Liszt's *Berlin Preludio*, S. 164g, is a two-bar album leaf signed in Berlin in 1842. Seven rolled chords rush into a compact C-major cadence, framed here like a page from a private album. #Shorts #piano
- **Format note:** pair it with another album leaf; eight seconds is not a viable standalone Short.

### 8. Tchaikovsky — Baba Yaga

- **Visual:** `sequin-gown`, midnight blue, silver glints, cel look, stronger vignette and dust. Fast single-frame director; hands, keyboard-up, low right, hammers and pedals.
- **Listening cue:** “Staccato footsteps keep the chase moving.”
- **Title:** **Baba Yaga Runs on Staccato | Tchaikovsky**
- **Description:** Tchaikovsky's *Baba Yaga*, Op. 39 No. 20, races through E minor at a Presto tempo. Midnight-blue sequins, sharp cel outlines and close views of the keys and hammers create a compact folktale chase. #Shorts #piano
