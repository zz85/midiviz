# Violin MIDI files for #037 Violinist

48 MIDI files for the violinist experiment: 32 movements of Bach's Sonatas and Partitas for solo violin (BWV 1001–1006, complete), 6 sonata movements with keyboard, 6 concerto movements and 4 showpieces. [index.json](index.json) lists them for a web page.

All files come from the [Mutopia Project](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/), whose pieces are engraved in LilyPond from public-domain editions and released under public domain or Creative Commons licences. The files are copied unchanged from Mutopia's downloads (the multi-movement zips were unpacked and one movement taken).

## What to know before using them

- **Every file is mechanical.** LilyPond writes the notation as MIDI: onsets on the grid, durations as written, one tempo per marking, no rubato. Most have a single velocity for every note; the Tchaikovsky finale, the Paganini caprices, Gounod and BWV 1042 turn the engraved dynamics into velocity steps (4 to 53 levels). No human-sequenced performance MIDI of these works with a free licence turned up (see *Not included*).
- **Find the violin by track index, not by program.** Ripieno violins in the concertos and the Beethoven Romance are also program 40. `violinTrack` in index.json is the track index in the SMF (track 0 is LilyPond's tempo track); `violinChannel` is 0-based.
- **Missing program changes.** BWV 1001, 1002 and 1004 have no program change at all, so a GM synth plays them as piano: force program 40 on the violin channel (`violinProgram: null` in index.json). The BWV 1017 keyboard tracks also lack one (piano by default, which is fine).
- **Tempo.** Movements engraved without a metronome mark play at LilyPond's default ♩=60, which is far off for many Bach movements (the C major Fuga takes 23.6 min as stored, the Gavotte en Rondeau plays at half speed). index.json has `bpm` (the file's first tempo) and, for those files, `suggestedBpm`: an approximate quarter-note tempo of typical recordings. Playback rate = suggestedBpm / bpm. BWV 1002 (Partita 1), BWV 1004 Giga and all the ensemble pieces have engraved tempos.
- **Repeats.** LilyPond only writes repeats into MIDI when the engraver unfolded them; the durations below are the files as stored.
- **Odd GM programs in the accompaniment.** Bach BWV 1042 / 1043 put ripieno strings and continuo on program 6 (Harpsichord); the Beethoven concerto and Romance put horns and trumpets on program 69 (English horn). Remap if the accompaniment should sound like the orchestra.
- **No merged parts.** In every file the solo violin has its own track and channel; no file mixes the violin with other parts. Double stops and chords in the Bach and Paganini pieces are written as simultaneous notes on the one violin track (up to 4).
- **Range.** Four files have a single note below the violin's open G (G3, MIDI 55), all engraving slips (see notes); the fingering engine should clamp or skip them. The Paganini caprices and the Beethoven / Tchaikovsky concertos go above C7 (to G7 = 103 in Caprice 1).

## Their scores: bowing from the source

The MIDI files carry no articulation: every note touches the next, and nothing says which notes share a bow. Mutopia's LilyPond sources do, so `violinist/scores/` holds the source of every piece, only the files each score reads (243 files, 1.2 MB; the same licences as the MIDI files). `index.json` gives each entry a `score`: its `main` file, the `files` it reads, the staff (`part`, `partName`) that is the MIDI's violin, and how well they agree (`matched`: the share of the MIDI's violin notes found in the score at the same pitch and place, 95.3–100 %, 99.5 % overall). #034, #037 and #038 read it with `strings/lilypond.js` and put its slurs, marks and bowings on the MIDI's notes (`violinist/score-art.js`), so the MIDI keeps its timing and the bow follows the score.

What the sources have:
- **Slurs: in every piece but one.** Of the note pairs, 0–60 % are slurred (20 % overall). Bach's Corrente BWV 1004 has 60 %, the Adagio BWV 1001 51 %, the C major Fuga 3 %. Handel's HWV 361 Andante has none: that edition left them out.
- **Articulation marks** (staccato, tenuto, accents) in the sonatas, concertos and showpieces: 4,000 marked notes. The Bach solo movements (Bach-Gesellschaft edition) have none.
- **Up- and down-bow signs: almost none.** Only the Tchaikovsky concerto has them (4 up, 68 down in the source). The other editions mark slurs and leave the bow direction to the player, as most do. The bow direction therefore still comes from alternating strokes, restarted on a down-bow after rests.

**Printed editions with bowings (IMSLP).** For Bach's Sonatas and Partitas, two performing editions add the bowings the urtext leaves out: Eduard Herrmann (G. Schirmer, 1900, IMSLP #335779) and Leopold Auer (Carl Fischer, 1917, IMSLP #921334), both public domain. Their scans are read by optical music recognition with ScoreShift (`../random-sketches/vibes/scoreshift`). It reads the notes, slurs, staccato / tenuto / accent marks and ⊓ / V bow signs on each page. The pitches are then aligned onto the MIDI's violin notes (5-note fingerprints to find the movement, then an edit-distance alignment), and the marks are stored per note in `violinist/editions/<edition>.json` (`node violinist/tools/build_editions.js`; the scans stay in the git-ignored `strings/data/IMSLP`). The results per edition, over the 32 movements:

| | Herrmann 1900 | Auer 1917 |
|---|---|---|
| Notes found | 91.5 % | 85.1 % |
| Slurred note pairs (Mutopia's BGA source on the same notes) | 5,594 (4,161) | 5,491 (3,708) |
| Mutopia's slurred pairs also slurred in the edition | 76.7 % (was 62.5) | 69.0 % (was 55.5) |
| Slur agreement with Mutopia, all pairs | 84.8 % | 79.1 % |
| Bow signs | 157 ⊓, 58 V | 275 ⊓, 226 V |

These figures are after ScoreShift learned to read slurs merged into note ink and bowing marks above beams (checked by eye on a few systems, see its README: Auer 22 → 25 of 36 slurs, Herrmann 12 of 12 with 1 false where it had 4, bowing marks 9 → 12 of about 14 on an Auer page). A slur running over a page turn is joined: a slur still open at the end of a page continues into the notes before the next page's first slur end. OMR still misses some marks: steep slurs that join two voices, and bowing marks fused with a beam. Herrmann mostly keeps Bach's slurs and marks bow strokes in the fugues and chords. Auer re-slurs more freely and marks bows throughout. In #037, Bowing: Herrmann / Auer appears for these movements. The bow signs set the stroke direction (`strings/motion.js`), which until now ignored them, the Tchaikovsky score's included.

`node violinist/tools/build_scores.js` rebuilds `violinist/scores` and the `score` entries from the sources (`node strings/test/fetch_mutopia_ly.js` fetches them into the git-ignored `strings/data/MutopiaLy`).

## Not included

Mutopia has none of them. The IMSLP work pages checked (Mendelssohn, Bruch, Brahms and Sibelius concertos, Beethoven Op. 24, Franck, Tartini, Elgar, Sarasate, Saint-Saëns, Monti, Massenet's Thaïs) list no MIDI with a violin part; Mendelssohn has only an MP3 accompaniment track (the Thaïs page's one MIDI, CC BY-SA 4.0 by Pierre Gouin, is an organ arrangement with no violin part); Wikimedia Commons only has short excerpts (a few bars of the Franck sonata opening, Berg's concerto melody, a Mozart cadenza).

## Reproducing the inspection

Each file was parsed twice in Node 26: with a small SMF parser (tracks, channels, program changes, note counts, ranges, polyphony, tempo map) and with `../../tonejs-midi.js` (`new Midi(buf)`); note counts and durations agree for all 48 files.
