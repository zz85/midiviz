# How commercial modelled violins get their sound

Research brief, 18 Oct 2026, for #034 ([03_violin_sound.md](03_violin_sound.md) is the wider survey). Question: what do Audio Modeling SWAM, Expressive E Soliste and Sound Magic Violin One do that our two physical models (`strings/sound/bowed.js`) do not?

None of the three publishes its internals. What follows comes from their manuals, their own technology pages, patents filed by the people behind SWAM, and reviews. Wherever a statement is an inference, it says so.

## 1. The three products

### 1.1 Audio Modeling SWAM Solo Strings (v3)

- **Pure physical modelling, no samples.** Sound On Sound: "While SWAM Woodwinds utilise a hybrid combination of modelling and sampling techniques, SWAM Solo Strings' sound is generated by pure physical modelling with no samples involved at all — hence the instruments' miniscule data footprint" ([review, p. 2](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/audio-modeling-swam-solo-instruments?page=2)). Audio Modeling's string pages say the same ("no samples", "physically modeled instruments do not work with samples at all"). The sample-based history (Lucato's SWT, and SWAM-W's "few megabytes" of samples) applies to the woodwinds, not the strings. Audio Modeling describes the engine as waveguide physical modelling plus behavioural modelling.
- **The body is a separate component.** The same KB article says the strings' "body emulations" were first researched by Giorgio Tommasini, then replaced by Audio Modeling's own in v2, and "are not part of the SWAM technology core". The manual's "Instruments" control selects "the main instrument timbre of the resonating body".
- **Related patents (Samplemodeling, not SWAM strings)** (Tommasini and Lucato, filed 2004, granted 2008, listed on [Samplemodeling's technology page](https://legacy.samplemodeling.com/tech)):
  1. "Time alignment of the phase of a set of musical sounds to be used with samplers": **harmonic alignment**. Recordings of one note at different dynamics are phase-aligned, so crossfading between them never comb-filters ("phasing").
  2. "Determination of modal resonances and body impulse response of a musical instrument by analysis of sounds performed with pitch changes. Application to the synthesis of vibrato & portamento with samplers": the **body is estimated from recordings with vibrato or glissandi**. As the pitch moves, each harmonic sweeps across the body response and traces it out. The body is then deconvolved out of the recordings and convolved back in at playback, after the pitch has been changed.

  Patents normally run 20 years from filing, so both have probably lapsed; I have not checked their status. These patents underlie Samplemodeling's sample-based instruments (The Stradivari Violin, Solo Strings), not SWAM Solo Strings. The second one is still useful to us: it shows how a body response can be estimated from vibrato and glissando recordings, data that SWAM's strings would need when their bodies were made. How Audio Modeling built its own bodies (v2 onward) is not published.
- **Behavioural modelling: the player is modelled too.** Audio Modeling: "not only do we model the mechanical system of an instrument, we also model the unconscious behaviors of a real professional musician" ([blog](https://audiomodeling.com/blog/we-are-the-future-of-digital-music-making)). The [SWAM Solo Strings manual](https://images.equipboard.com/uploads/item/manual/109721/audio-modeling-swam-solo-strings-bundle-manual.pdf) lists what that covers:
  - Staccato, legato, portamento and bow change are chosen by "a special algorithm" from note velocity, the interval, and the time between note-off and the next note-on.
  - **Interactive BowPress** "controls the amount of correction of the bow pressure to maintain a good vibrating sound". This is a feedback loop that keeps the string in its playable (Helmholtz) regime.
  - Random Bow (pressure, speed and position), Random Finger (pitch), Vibrato Random, and Vibrato Fade-In.
  - "Dynamic Transit." shapes the loudness during legato transitions.
  - The portamento split ratio sets where on the interval a cross-string slide changes string.
  - "Mono String Crossing" stops the old string's ring on small intervals, so two notes do not overlap.
  - Pan "Dyn1/Dyn2" "mimics little movements of the player".
- **Controls exposed:**
  - Expression is read as "the speed of the bow in relation to the bow pressure".
  - Bow pressure (low gives flautando, high gives scratch), bow position, rosin, string resonance, bow noise, sympathetic "FreeStrings" and brightness.
  - Harmonics, sordino, and a bow-lift state on the release.
  - v3 adds a 4th harmonic, Timbral Correction, "Virtual Single String", EQ, early reflections and the "Ambiente" room ([v3 notes](https://kb.audiomodeling.com/support/solutions/articles/206000041686-swam-solo-strings-v3-main-features-improvements)).
- **Its own advice:** use a continuous expression controller, because the instrument does not work without one. Also keep most playing in the mp–mf range, because "too low an output level would easily induce the user to use high Expression levels, usually yielding excessively 'biting' sounds."

### 1.2 Expressive E Soliste (2025)

- Soliste is a physically modelled violin, viola, cello and bass. It evolved from Expressive E's Arché Collection, needs about 400 MB per instrument, and has no double stops ([press release](https://expressive-e.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/press/Soliste_PR.pdf), [Sound On Sound review](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/expressive-e-soliste)).
- **Bodies:** a choice of "resonant body" timbres (six for the violin: Spark, Hollow, Sleek, Antique, Lyric, Mellow) and "selecting a different body … like switching between various instruments". A 400 MB footprint for a model suggests large body data such as convolution responses; that is an inference.
- **Gesture-first control:** Classic mode taps for a bow stroke and presses for sustain intensity. Virtuoso mode maps back-and-forth gestures to the bow. There is also an assisted vibrato that fades in, with a smooth hand-over to manual vibrato.
- **Exposed bow parameters:** attack time and gesture shape, plus five hidden "advanced" parameters for bow position, pressure and noise. The articulations (sul ponticello, flautando, spiccato, martelé, tremolo) are just presets of these.
- **The review's verdict:** it is very playable, but "straightforward expressive melodic playing exhibits a certain quite strong synthetic quality". The tone becomes "quite flute‑ or tube‑like in some tessitura", and legato has "a little whiff of synth‑y portamento". Sample libraries "often triumph in sheer naturalistic accuracy" while modellers win on feel.

### 1.3 Sound Magic Violin One (free, 2025)

- Violin One "combines physical modeling with behavior modeling", which they contrast with their usual "Hybrid Modeling" sample engines. It is 20 MB, with MPE / MIDI 2.0, "Embodied Microphone Modeling" and a 3D panner ([product page](https://neovst.com/violin-one/)). Its controls are dynamic range, attack, sustain, legato and modelling timbre, plus a virtual stage ([BPB](https://bedroomproducersblog.com/2025/04/18/sound-magic-violin-one/)). No technical detail beyond that is published.

## 2. What they share

1. **A real body.** All three treat the body as a separate, swappable component (Soliste's 400 MB suggests large body data), and Samplemodeling patented a way to estimate one from recordings. A violin body has hundreds of resonances, and their density above 1 kHz is what makes wood sound like wood.
2. **No recordings in the source of the modelled strings.** SWAM Solo Strings, Soliste and Violin One synthesise the string itself. Only Samplemodeling's instruments (a different company) are built from samples. So a pure model can reach SWAM's level; the gap is in how good the model and its body are.
3. **A model of the player.** This covers transitions picked from interval, timing and velocity, randomness in bow and finger, assisted vibrato, and keeping the bow inside its playable window by feedback.
4. **Space and movement:** early reflections, a room, microphone modelling, and small player movements that change the radiated tone over time.
5. **A pure model can still sound synthetic.** SOS found Soliste not naturalistic in melodic playing, while SWAM is widely considered the most realistic modelled violin. Both are pure models, so the difference lies in their bodies, bow models and behaviour, which neither publishes.

## 3. Our physical models against that list

| Ingredient | SWAM / Soliste / Violin One | #034 waveguide and source |
|---|---|---|
| Body | Measured or estimated from recordings, selectable | 10 signature modes + 42 random "dense" modes from 1 to 7.5 kHz per ear (`Body` in bowed.js), then a 13-band EQ fitted to VSCO. Far too few modes: likely the main cause of the hollow, "plasticky" tone. |
| Source micro-detail | Modelled (bow noise, rosin, string resonance controls in SWAM) | Pure model: 1.5 % rosin jitter on μs; a sawtooth plus gated noise |
| Bow kept playable | Interactive BowPress (feedback) | Feed-forward only: F ≥ 0.02·(Z/0.3)^1.5·v/β². Missed Helmholtz motion on 26 of 369 pitch × dynamic cases in our sweep when it was tuned (wrong octave or raucous); the test only asks for 85 %. |
| Behaviour | Transitions from interval, timing and velocity; random bow and finger; dynamic transitions | `performance.js` + `humanize.js` cover most of this already |
| String crossings in slurs | Old string's ring stopped on small intervals | Force hands over within 12 ms; old string rings freely |
| Radiation and space | Directivity, mic models, player movement, ER + room | Static stereo (two random mode sets) + the #030 hall |
| Controls in mp–mf | Advised | Our default bow force targets the middle of the window, often bright and "biting" |

## 4. What to change, in order

1. **A measured body.** Two routes:
   - **(a)** Estimate it from recordings we already have, as the 2004 patent does: track each harmonic's amplitude across the vibrato and glissando samples (VSCO "Arco Vib", University of Iowa) to get the body's magnitude response densely, then build a minimum-phase FIR of about 2,048 taps per ear. That costs one `ConvolverNode`, or a partitioned convolution in the worklet. Pérez Carrillo, Bonada, Pätynen and Välimäki measured violin radiation from bowed glissandi with exactly this kind of deconvolution ([JASA 2011](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article/130/2/1020/957729/Method-for-measuring-violin-sound-radiation-based-on-bowed-glissandi-and-its-application-to-sound-synthesis), [Pérez PhD](https://www.mtg.upf.edu/static/media/Perez-Alfonso-PhD-2009.pdf)).
   - **(b)** Use the NBody hammer impulse responses (Cook and Trueman, Princeton, 1998): a violin measured at 12 microphone directions, per string ([files](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~prc/NBody/violin/)). They carry no licence, so they would stay git-ignored like the TNUA and VSCO data unless the authors agree.

   Either route also gives directivity: crossfade slowly between directions to mimic player movement.
2. **Bow-pressure feedback (Interactive BowPress).** In the waveguide, count slip events per period, which is cheap because we already know when the string slips. Two slips per period means a double-slip or wrong octave: raise the force. Irregular slips mean raucous: lower it. The fix should be smoothed over about 20 ms and limited to a few dB. This targets the wrong-octave and scratch artifacts directly.
3. **Richer modelled bow noise.** Follow SWAM's bow-noise, rosin and string-resonance controls: noise from the friction itself (shaped by slip speed and force), a second string polarisation, and torsion. Keep it all synthetic, as SWAM does. (Mixing in recorded attacks is the hybrid sampler's job, engine 6.)
4. **Behaviour gaps:** stop the old string's ring on slurred crossings of small intervals; keep the default bow force lower in the window (mp–mf); add Vibrato Random and Random Bow as continuous slow noise rather than per-note offsets.
5. **Early reflections before the hall,** at short delays and slightly coloured, so the dry model never sounds anechoic.

Items 1 and 2 are probably most of the audible gap; the others are refinements. Item 1 uses recordings only to measure the body (a filter), not as sound, so the engine stays a pure physical model like SWAM's.
